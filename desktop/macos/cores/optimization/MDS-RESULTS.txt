MDS / Midstate - Mac 0.1.14 scheduling optimization
Measured 2026-10-16 on one base Apple M3, 10 GPU cores, 16 GiB.

Retained changes
- Auto batch size resolves to 2560 only for the exact Apple M3 / Metal adapter.
  Other adapters retain 2048. Explicit nonzero batch settings take precedence.
- Metal completion polling sleeps 500 microseconds between checks. It does not
  busy-spin or return until wgpu reports that GPU submissions have retired.
- 2000 iterations/dispatch, 64 threads/group, two CPU verification threads and
  the GUI's 90% requested duty remain. Stop/quit and the 0.5% fee are unchanged.
- BLAKE3 WGSL is byte-identical to 0.1.13; its SHA256 is
  0c70b8c8cdb529c7dcd37bf2c7a13a9c1f14c3071de7510186623268c2397dc3.
  Every nonce still executes the full million rounds; every GPU candidate is
  checked by the CPU. No research shader override or experimental policy ships.

Controlled comparison of the actual signed production binaries
Order: old, new, new, old; 90 seconds each, same M3 and 90% duty.
Old: 2048 nonces, blocking completion wait. New: 2560, sleeping completion poll.
Each process completed the original CPU and Metal selftests before timing.

                 Old A1      New B1      New B2      Old A2
Work rate N/s    358.429     475.121     477.054     360.142
CPU % / 1 core     5.8         7.1         7.3         6.4

Mean work rate: 359.286 -> 476.088 N/s (+32.51%).
Completed-batch compute rate: 358.887 -> 475.813 N/s.
Mean time per complete batch: 5.707 -> 5.380 seconds.
Median samples after the first 30 seconds: 358.663 -> 474.828 N/s.
These are local full-chain work equivalents, including fractional progress in
the work-rate counter. They are not pool-estimated hashrate or revenue claims.
CPU percentages use macOS's one-core=100% convention, not whole-machine usage.

An additional 45.014-second offline test with an easy 10-bit share target ran
at 478.603 N/s and produced 16 CPU-verified proofs. This did not use a network
or produce pool-accepted shares. Real GUI/pool results and installer integrity
are recorded separately in gui/evidence/mac-mds-optimization-0.1.14.json.

Rejected experiments / limitations
- Specialized 32-byte BLAKE3, struct states, reordered additions, loop unrolling,
  vector4 expressions and interleaved independent chains did not establish a
  useful repeatable shader improvement. Original shader retained.
- 4096/4000 configurations sometimes began near 800 N/s then fell to about
  275 N/s after roughly 20 seconds. Their short averages are misleading and
  those profiles were excluded. Other large batches also lost useful latency.
- QoS changes, NSProcessInfo activities, sleep-credit pacing and grouping short
  passes did not reliably eliminate that cliff. None is enabled in the release.
- 2560/2000 performed better than tested 3072/2000, 5120/1500 and 2560/3000
  alternatives while retaining short batch completion time. The mechanism of
  the large-dispatch cliff is not proven by hardware counters.
- No claim of all-M-series performance, multi-day stability, power efficiency
  or guaranteed pool returns. Other adapter names use the conservative profile.
  The untested 8-core M3 reports the same name and also selects the M3 profile;
  the measured result here applies only to the tested 10-core machine.

Validation
45 GUI/controller tests on Windows and Mac; 11 native protocol tests;
2 profile-selection tests; 2 CPU consensus tests; 2 actual GPU tests.
GPU tests include full-chain equality, a sparse comparison at batch-size minus
three with nonce low-word carry and a partial workgroup, candidate capacity,
and CPU verification/cancellation across jobs. Existing source licenses remain.
